01 The Friendship

Barbara & Luis

A bond of nearly 25 years

For nearly a quarter century, Barbara Meyer de Garate and Luis Barragán shared a deep and enduring friendship. She was among his closest confidantes — someone who understood both the man and the architect, who appreciated the silence in his buildings and the warmth in his character.

By 1978, Barragán was 76 years old. His health was declining, and he knew his years of active practice were drawing to a close. For one of his dearest friends, he chose to design one final home — a last act of architectural devotion, a gift rendered in light, color, and stone.

"Para Baby Meyer de Garate, con todo cariño"

For Baby Meyer de Garate, with all affection

— inscribed on the original facade drawings, October 31, 1978

02 The Architect

Luis Barragán

1902 — 1988

Luis Ramiro Barragán Morfín was a Mexican architect and engineer whose work has profoundly influenced the trajectory of modern architecture. Born in Guadalajara, Jalisco, Barragán developed a unique architectural language that fused modernist principles with the rich traditions of Mexican vernacular architecture.

His buildings are characterized by bold planes of color — vivid pinks, deep purples, warm yellows, and burnt oranges — set against austere geometric forms. He manipulated natural light and water to create spaces of profound emotional and spiritual resonance, what he called "emotional architecture."

1980 Pritzker Architecture Prize Laureate
2004 Casa Luis Barragán declared UNESCO World Heritage Site
03 The House

Casa Meyer

Designed October 31, 1978

Casa Meyer stands as Luis Barragán's final residential project — a culminating expression of decades spent refining an architecture of silence, color, and emotional depth. More than a commission, it was a gift to one of his dearest friends, conceived as both a home and a meditation on the relationship between architecture and landscape.

Located in Mexico City, the house embodies Barragán's mature vision: massive walls that filter and direct light, gardens that blur the boundary between interior and exterior, and a color palette drawn from Mexican tradition — the hot pink of bougainvillea, the warm yellow of colonial facades, the white of lime-washed adobe.

A Gift Between Friends

After nearly 25 years of friendship, Barragán designed this home at age 76 as a final act of devotion — every element serves both functional and poetic purpose.

Light & Shadow

The interplay of natural light with colored walls creates an ever-changing interior landscape, transforming the home throughout the day.

Garden Integration

Lush vegetation cascades over the austere white walls, creating a dialogue between the geometric and the organic that defines Barragán's work.

04 Architecture

Facades & Form

The original drawings, signed and dated October 31, 1978, reveal Barragán's meticulous approach to composition. Each facade is a study in geometric restraint — stacked volumes, carefully proportioned openings, and the interplay of solid and void.

The Pink Lattice

One of Barragán's signature elements — a geometric screen that filters light and creates shifting patterns throughout the day. The lattice at Casa Meyer, rendered in his characteristic hot pink, transforms a simple grid into a living element that breathes with the passage of the sun.
